## Ad-safe rewrites

Meta and TikTok both restrict paid promotion of skin bleaching, and treatment claims can reclassify a cosmetic as a drug. Left column is what the market says; right column is what you can safely say.

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| Premium skin brightening | Evens tone and brings out natural glow |
| Reduce wrinkles / anti-ageing | Leaves skin feeling smooth and supple |
| Anti stretch marks | Conditions and softens skin |
| SPF 30 on a wash-off product | Do not repeat this claim in marketing — see note below |

## Notes

**The SPF 30 mark needs scrutiny.** A rinse-off shower gel cannot deliver meaningful sun protection — SPF is measured on leave-on products at a set application density. Whatever the label says, do not build marketing around it, and never tell a customer this replaces sunscreen. If asked on camera, the honest answer is that sun protection comes from a separate sunscreen. This is the single most likely claim to attract regulatory attention.

**"13 in 1" needs a definitive list.** Right now nobody publicly knows what the thirteen are. Getting that list from the factory and publishing it is a genuine content win — it is the kind of detail resellers cannot supply.

## Verify before publishing

Everything above was assembled from third-party retailer listings, not from SK Duchess. Treat it as a draft to correct, not a source of truth.

**Ask the factory for:**

- [ ] Exact product name as printed on the label
- [ ] Net volume and any other sizes produced
- [ ] Official wholesale and recommended retail price
- [ ] Full INCI ingredient list from the carton
- [ ] NAFDAC registration number
- [ ] Batch code format and where it is printed
- [ ] Manufacture and expiry date conventions
- [ ] Whether this variant is current, discontinued, or reformulated
