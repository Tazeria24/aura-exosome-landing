# SK Duchess — Product Catalogue

A working catalogue of every SK Duchess product traceable in public retail, compiled September 2026.

**Read this first: none of it came from SK Duchess.** Every detail below was assembled from third-party retailer listings, marketplace pages and product photography, because the brand publishes nothing itself. That absence is the whole problem — and the whole opportunity.

Treat these files as a draft to take to the factory and correct, not as a record of truth. Each one ends with a checklist of what only your father's business can confirm.

## What the catalogue reveals

### The range is deep, and almost nobody knows it

Fourteen products. Most customers can name one, maybe two. Retailers stock a handful each and describe them inconsistently. A buyer who loves the 13-in-1 has no way to discover that a gentler honey version exists, or a citrus one, or a retinol one for body acne.

**There is no map of your own range anywhere on the internet.** Publishing one is the single cheapest, highest-return thing the brand can do online. It costs nothing but accuracy.

### Prices are out of control

The same 1000 ml bottle sells between ₦7,850 and ₦12,000 depending on the retailer. That is a spread of more than fifty per cent on identical goods. Customers who notice feel cheated, and the brand reads as cheap where it is discounted and as a rip-off where it is not.

An official published price list would end this in a week.

### Two products are confusingly close

**Luminous Glow Super L-Glutathione** and **Super L-Glutathione 13-in-1** are listed interchangeably by retailers, sometimes ₦4,000 apart. Customers cannot distinguish them from a thumbnail. Either differentiate them clearly or consolidate them.

The same likely applies to the **Vitamin C & Collagen Scrub** against the **Turmeric** version, and to the **Retinol Skin Repair Wash** against the **Retinol Rose 3-in-1**.

### Two variants can carry paid advertising; the rest cannot

Meta and TikTok both restrict paid promotion of skin bleaching. Most of this range leads with brightening or whitening language, which means paid ads will be rejected — and repeated rejections put the whole ad account at risk.

**Blood Orange Blossom** and **Honey Sweet Secret** sell on scent and gentleness rather than lightening. If money is ever put behind SK Duchess, it goes behind those two. Everything else stays organic.

### One claim should be removed immediately

The body lotion is marketed as **"7 Days"**. A named timeframe is specific, testable, and falsifiable. It invites regulatory attention, refund demands, and — worst of all — the "I used it for 7 days and nothing happened" video, which is exactly the format TikTok rewards. Every disappointed customer is handed a ready-made attack.

Nothing else in the catalogue carries this much downside for this little upside.

### The best ingredient stories are buried

Ceramide in the honey wash. Arbutin in the milk scrub. Turmeric, which Nigerian customers already trust without being sold. These are real, credible, zero-risk stories, and the marketing barely mentions them — because it is spending all its language on brightening.

## What only the factory can answer

These questions block real work. Get them answered once and most of this catalogue becomes authoritative:

1. **The definitive SKU list.** Which of these fourteen are current, discontinued, or renamed?
2. **What are the 13 in "13 in 1"?** Nobody knows. Publishing the list is a content win no reseller can copy.
3. **NAFDAC registration numbers** for each product.
4. **Full INCI lists** from the cartons — only the honey variant's is public.
5. **Batch code format** and where it is printed. This is the foundation of the authenticity checker.
6. **Official wholesale and retail prices.**
7. **Is the Face Cream ours?** Some listings appear to bundle a third-party cream.
8. **Retinol Skin Repair Wash vs Retinol Rose 3-in-1** — one product or two?
9. **Manufactured or distributed?** Sources conflict on whether the brand is Thai in origin. This decides whether your story is "we make this" or "we bring you the real thing", and whether factory footage is available at all.
