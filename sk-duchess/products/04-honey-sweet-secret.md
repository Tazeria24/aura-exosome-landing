## What it is

A honey-and-milk wash. This is the only variant with a full ingredient list circulating publicly, which makes it the easiest to speak about precisely.

## Key ingredients

- **Honey extract** — The signature ingredient; humectant and soothing.
- **Hydrolysed milk protein** — Conditioning, creamy lather.
- **Ceramide** — Supports the skin barrier — a genuinely good story, and underused in the marketing.
- **Propolis** — Bee-derived; listed as a natural antiseptic.
- **Citric acid** — pH adjustment.

## Ad-safe rewrites

Meta and TikTok both restrict paid promotion of skin bleaching, and treatment claims can reclassify a cosmetic as a drug. Left column is what the market says; right column is what you can safely say.

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| Natural antiseptic | Gently cleansing — avoid "antiseptic", which reads as a medical claim |

## Notes

**Two things to flag honestly.**

The preservative system is methylchloroisothiazolinone / methylisothiazolinone (MCI/MI). These are permitted in rinse-off products but are a known contact allergen, and the EU restricts them tightly. For a rinse-off gel this is legal and common, but if you ever export or someone reacts, you need to know it is there. Worth asking the factory whether a reformulation is planned.

**Ceramide is buried and shouldn't be.** "Contains ceramide" is a barrier-care story that the whole beauty market currently understands and rewards, and it carries no regulatory risk at all. This variant is being undersold.
